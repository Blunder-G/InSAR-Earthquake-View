Dancing with the SARs

How radar from space revealed the ground moving during the February 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes (Türkiye)

The story

On 6 February 2023, two very large earthquakes struck southern Türkiye and northern Syria within about nine hours: a magnitude 7.8 event followed by a magnitude 7.5 event. The ground along the fault system shifted, and communities across the region were severely affected.

Satellites can show us this movement from space. Radar works day and night and sees through clouds, so it can map the damage zone when optical cameras cannot.

What the radar saw

We compared two Sentinel-1 radar images of the same area: 29 January 2023 (before) and 10 February 2023 (after). The result is an interferogram, a map in which colour shows how much the ground moved between the two dates.

How to read the picture

One full colour cycle ≈ 2.8 cm of movement along the satellite's line of sight
Tight ringsMany colour cycles packed together means the ground moved a lot over a short distance.
Broad, smooth colourLittle movement, as in the corners far from the fault.
White or grey patchesLakes (masked out) or places where the radar signal did not match between dates.
Two main ring clustersThe picture shows two distinct zones of strong deformation, one in the north and one in the south.

Our interpretation

The dense rings mark where the fault system ruptured and the surface shifted. The presence of more than one cluster is consistent with the earthquake sequence involving more than one fault segment, and with the two large shocks that happened within hours of each other. Both fall inside our 12-day window, so this single image shows their combined effect.

Limits: InSAR measures movement along one line of sight, not the full 3-D motion. This image also cannot separate the first shock from the second.

Why it matters, and what comes next

Maps like this help responders see where the ground moved most, support damage assessment, and improve hazard models for future events. NISAR, the NASA-ISRO radar satellite, uses longer-wavelength L-band radar and observes the whole planet regularly, which will make this kind of change mapping routine, including over forested and vegetated land where shorter wavelengths lose signal.

Data and method

  • Sentinel-1A radar scenes, 29 Jan 2023 and 10 Feb 2023, found with ASF Data Search Vertex.
  • Interferogram produced with ASF's HyP3 on-demand InSAR (GAMMA) service.
  • Water mask applied; displacement and wrapped-phase products also generated.
